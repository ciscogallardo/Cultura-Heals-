Grounding · 2–9 minutes · 2 of 10

Our grounding: two hummingbird teachings

First story

Mexica Tiahui

Huitzilopochtli, represented through the hummingbird, guides the Mexica on their journey. When the people are weary and discouraged, the call is to keep moving forward.

Purpose · ancestral guidance · perseverance
Second story

The hummingbird and the forest fire

A hummingbird carries what water it can toward a fire threatening the forest. It cannot do everything, but it can do something.

Responsibility · limits · survival · balance

How do we care for our community without losing sight of what we need to survive and heal?

Facilitator notes — Cisco (tap to expand)

First teaching: ancestral guidance and perseverance — Mexica Tiahui. Second teaching: the hummingbird acts with what it has, while the fire threatens what sustains it. Emphasize responsibility, survival and balance: healing includes caring for ourselves as we care for others.

Recognize what happened

Remember what is present

Four movements · 26–33 minutes · 7 of 10

The healing journey

1 · Acknowledgment

Who am I? Who do I represent? What is my purpose?

2 · Understanding

Make meaning of change, disruption, challenges and opportunities.

3 · Integration

Recognize cargas and regalos — burdens and gifts — in family and community experiences.

4 · Movement

Put values, teachings, creativity and solutions into action.

Facilitator notes — Cisco (tap to expand)

Key points: Acknowledgment asks who we are, who we represent and our purpose. Understanding explores challenges and growth. Integration recognizes burdens and gifts. Movement puts teachings, values and solutions into practice.

From círculo to casework · 33–39 minutes · 8 of 10

What does this look like with young people?

In a círculo

Begin with relationships, belonging, listening, shared agreements and culturally meaningful reflection.

In casework

Ask about strengths, family, community and purpose — not only risk, problems or compliance.

In youth development

Support identity, life skills, leadership, meaningful roles and connections to elders.

In community

Build safe, compassionate relationships and places where young people can grow and heal.

Facilitator notes — Cisco (tap to expand)

Key points: Talking and teaching circles strengthen relationships; rites of passage develop identity, life skills and leadership. Name Circle Keeper, Joven Noble, Girasol and Cara y Corazón as examples from the presentation.
